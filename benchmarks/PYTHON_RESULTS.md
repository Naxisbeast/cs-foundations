## The surprise — where theory and measurement disagreed

**merge_sort and `sorted()` are both O(n log n), yet at n = 1M our pure-Python merge sort takes 2.6 seconds and `sorted()` takes 24 milliseconds — roughly a 100× difference.** Big O predicted the same growth rate; the stopwatch shows the constant factor is the whole story. `sorted()` is C; our merge sort is interpreted Python, and every element visit carries interpreter overhead that asymptotic notation doesn't capture.

This is the honest reason the hand-written versions exist: not to compete with the standard library, but to understand what the standard library is doing — and to know that "O(n log n)" says nothing about which one is 100× faster.

## What the measurement did confirm

- **binary_search vs `list.index()`: the growth curves match theory.** At n = 1M, index takes 16 ms (linear) while binary search is unmeasurably small (logarithmic). The gap widens with n exactly as O(log n) vs O(n) predicts.
- **Stack and queue scale linearly** (O(1) per operation) — 0.0003s at 1k to ~0.3s at 1M is a 1000× cost for 1000× the work. `collections.deque` is marginally ahead of the list-based stack, as expected.
